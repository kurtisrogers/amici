What this is

{{.Brand.Name}} is Latin and Italian for "friends", which is the whole specification. It is a place to tell the people you actually know what you have been up to, and to hear the same back from them. That is the entire feature list, and we are quite deliberate about keeping it that way.

How people find each other

There are exactly two ways, and there will not be a third.

  1. You already know their email address. Send a request to it. We will never tell you whether that address belongs to an account here, because being able to check is itself the thing we are preventing.
  2. They gave you a request code. A member generates a code and passes it on however they like. It works once and stops working after twenty four hours, or four hours if they are under eighteen.

There is no search box. There is no suggestion engine. There is no "friends of friends", no contact upload, no phone number matching and no profile link you can post publicly. Every one of those is a way for a stranger to arrive, so none of them exists.

Who can see your posts

Confirmed friends, or nobody but you. Those are the only two audiences {{.Brand.Name}} can express. There is no public post, no unlisted post and no link that lets somebody outside see in.

Your friend list is yours alone. Nobody, friend or not, can browse it, and neither support nor developers can read it.

Looking after younger members

You must be at least thirteen to join. Members under eighteen cannot be reached by anybody who merely knows their email address, whatever their settings say: a code they chose to hand over is the only route to them, and their codes expire in four hours rather than twenty four.

Nobody can find them, nobody can suggest them to a stranger, and no stranger can see anything they post. There are no profile photographs anywhere on {{.Brand.Name}}, for anybody.

Your profile page

You can write the HTML and CSS of your own profile, the way the web worked before every page looked the same. Fonts, colours, marquees, blinking text, a wonky table layout if that is what you want.

Everything you write goes through a sanitiser that keeps decoration and removes anything that could run, phone home or dress itself up as {{.Brand.Name}} asking for your password. Your page is then displayed inside a sandbox that cannot run script even if something slipped through. We would rather explain why we removed something than let one clever profile read a visitor's account.

Accounts with extra powers

Three kinds of account exist, and the limits matter more than the powers.

  • Members. People. Almost every account.
  • Support. Can find an account from an email address to help somebody back in, can suspend an account, can act on reports, and can switch a profile's customisation back to plain. Cannot read posts, comments, friend lists or anybody's markup.
  • Developers. Can see whether the service is healthy and what the schema is doing. Cannot read member content either.

Every privileged action is written to an audit record with the name of the person who took it, and they can see that record on their own settings page. Power that leaves a trail is power people use carefully.

Money

There are no adverts, so nothing about you needs to be measured, packaged or sold. The intention is to cover the cost of running the thing and stop there. An {{.Brand.Name}} that needed to grow would start making the decisions that ruined everything else.
